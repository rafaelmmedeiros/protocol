bearing: Four direct trials contrast two implementations of the *same* movement — cable vs barbell preacher curl, dumbbell vs cable lateral raise, preacher vs Bayesian curl, incline vs flat press — and whole-muscle growth is null in all of them, once with an extreme Bayes factor in trained lifters. Implementation moves *regional* thickness and *tested strength*, not muscle size. A movement/variant split earns nothing on the growth side, and the muscle map cannot live on a parent row.

**What is claimed.** That two implementations of the same movement — barbell, dumbbell, cable,
selectorised machine — are training-equivalent and differ only in convenience; or, in the
opposing version, that one implementation is *better* because it holds tension across the whole
range while the other unloads at a joint angle, and that a catalogue should therefore rank
variants.

**What the evidence actually shows.** Four trials hold the movement constant and change only the
implementation. This is a much narrower contrast than the free-weights-versus-machines pooling in
`references/exercise-selection-within-a-movement-pattern.md`, and it is the contrast the question
actually asks about.

*The "constant tension" claim has a direct test, and it returns a null.* Nunes et al. (2020) is
the cable-versus-barbell preacher curl trial: 35 untrained young adults, 10 weeks, 3 sessions a
week, 3 sets of 8-12, one group on a cable (torque maintained where the barbell unloads) and one
on a barbell (torque concentrated at the extended, lengthened position). Biceps thickness rose 7%
with the cable and 8% with the barbell, p=0.346. The only significant difference ran the *other*
way from the constant-tension prediction: elbow flexion peak torque at 20 degrees of flexion
improved +39% barbell versus +30% cable (p=0.046), with 60 degrees (p=0.874) and 100 degrees
(p=0.728) null. The variant that "loses tension at the top" produced the one advantage found, at
the angle where it loads hardest.

*And it returns a null in trained lifters, with an emphatic statistic.* Larsen et al. (2025): 24
resistance-trained participants, mean 7.1 years of experience, within-participant design — one arm
cable lateral raise (torque greatest at long lengths), the other dumbbell (torque greatest at short
lengths), 8 weeks, 5 sets per limb per session, 12-16 reps to momentary failure, ROM standardised.
Distal deltoid +4.6% cable versus +3.9% dumbbell (BF 0.27); proximal +3.3% versus +3.4% (BF 0.22);
the multivariate analysis returned **BF below 0.01 — "extreme" evidence *for* the null.** This is
the single most relevant trial in the corpus for the variant question: same movement, same ROM,
same effort, opposite resistance profiles, trained subjects, no difference anywhere measured.

*Matching the profile removes what is left.* Attarieh et al. (2025), 15 untrained men, 10 weeks,
within-subject: preacher curl versus Bayesian cable curl, deliberately matched for resistance
profile (both peaking near 60 degrees of elbow flexion) so that only shoulder angle differed.
Thickness rose 6-9% at every biceps region in both, no differences between conditions or regions;
1RM +28% versus +37%, reported as equivalent. Their conclusion is the useful one: once resistance
profile, ROM and effort are matched, the residual geometric difference between two variants does
nothing.

*Accommodating resistance — the strongest version of "constant tension" — is null for size and
positive only for maximal strength.* The 2023 systematic review of variable versus conventional
resistance (12 studies) concluded variable resistance does not produce greater muscle mass over
short-to-medium periods in untrained subjects. The strength meta-analysis (Liu 2022) does report a
substantial pooled advantage on maximal strength (ES 0.80, 95% CI 0.42-1.19) — but that is a 1RM
outcome, typically with bands or chains on a barbell at 80%+ 1RM, and it is not a hypertrophy
claim.

*Where implementation does move something, it is regional, and it is small.* Chaves et al. (2020),
47 untrained men, 8 weeks, horizontal versus incline versus combined bench press: at the 2nd
intercostal space (upper pectoralis) the incline group gained 0.62 cm more than horizontal
(p=0.003) and 0.50 cm more than combined (p=0.008); at the 3rd (p=0.095) and 5th (p=0.227)
intercostal spaces there was nothing, and isometric strength did not differ (~10-11 kg in all).
Kassiano et al. (2025), 63 untrained women, 8 weeks: incline curl produced greater *proximal*
elbow flexor thickness, preacher curl greater *distal*, mid-muscle similar — differences the
authors describe as consistent and statistically significant but quantitatively modest. Both
trials found strength gains followed specificity: each group improved most on the variant it
trained.

*The mechanism, stated correctly.* "Tension the whole time" conflates two things. External force
constancy is not joint torque constancy — torque is force times a moment arm that changes with
joint angle. A barbell preacher curl unloads at the top because the resistance moment arm
approaches zero when the forearm passes vertical; a cam-profiled machine attempts to track the
strength curve; a cable moves the zero elsewhere rather than abolishing it. **So the intuition
points at something real — variants differ in where in the range the hard part falls — but the
trials above are precisely the ones that manipulated that, and whole-muscle growth did not
follow.**

*Taxonomy precedent is thin and one-level.* Wu et al. (2024) propose an eight-pattern movement
taxonomy for surveillance and state explicitly that it classifies by dominant joint action
*independent of implementation* — then concede that resistance type "may alter categorisation" and
that free-weight exercises often need placement in more than one category. The 2025 Sports Medicine
review of exercise classification reports mixed accuracy and calls for standardisation. **Nothing
found formalises a two-level movement-to-implementation hierarchy as a data model**; published
taxonomies are one level, and commercial catalogues are flat rows with an equipment tag.

**What it does not settle.**

- **Pools of one to four trials, mostly untrained, 8-10 weeks.** Larsen 2025 is the only trained
  sample and it is n=24 within-participant. Convergent nulls on a narrow contrast, not a
  demonstrated zero.
- **Selectorised and cam-profiled machines are barely tested in the within-movement contrast.**
  Nunes tested a cable, Larsen a cable, Attarieh a cable. **No trial found compares a selectorised
  preacher-curl machine to a barbell preacher curl.** The nearest evidence is the cable contrast,
  which is null, plus Haugen's pooled machine-versus-free-weight null.
- **Regional hypertrophy is real and its practical meaning is unknown.** Whether Chaves' 0.62 cm at
  one of three sites accumulates into anything visible over years, and whether a mixed catalogue
  captures it automatically, is untested.
- **Muscle length may be the axis that matters, and it is separately contested.** Kassiano 2025's
  incline-versus-preacher split is a *length* contrast dressed as a variant contrast; Attarieh's
  null, where profile was matched and only shoulder angle varied, cuts against it.
  `references/muscle-length-and-exercise-variant.md` is unchanged by anything here.
- **Nothing tests stability demand as an isolated variable.** The best proxy is Haugen's null,
  which is the largest stability contrast the literature offers.
- **ROM afforded by an implementation is asserted everywhere and measured nowhere.** Larsen 2025
  standardised ROM between conditions, which removes the variable rather than testing it.
- **The muscle map does change by variant, and no trial quantifies by how much.**

**Provenance caveat.** The Kassiano 2025 effect sizes came through a secondary summary (the
publisher returned 403); direction and the specificity finding are corroborated, the magnitudes
are not. The Attarieh 1RM figures come from a single page fetch and should be spot-checked if
anything turns on them.

**Where it touches the product.**

- **It closes the movement/variant split as a *growth* question.** No property a variant row would
  carry has been shown to change hypertrophy. Recorded as `TD-015`.
- **It is the reason the muscle map cannot live on a movement.** Regional maps differ by variant
  (Chaves), and `TD-005` already relies on that. An abstraction whose most important attribute
  cannot be inherited is not an abstraction.
- **It sharpens, rather than overturns,
  `references/exercise-selection-within-a-movement-pattern.md`.** That note nulls modality across
  pooled comparisons; this nulls implementation within a single movement, which is narrower and
  stronger. Both leave regional adaptation and tested strength open.
- **`stability_demand` stays omitted (`TD-005`), and the omission is now better supported.**
- **A general "this variant is better for growth" ranking is not supportable.** See
  `references/ranking-exercise-variants.md`.
