**What is claimed.** That because volume is the dominant hypertrophy driver with a monotonic
dose-response, the right long-run progression for a hypertrophy programme is to add **sets**
over a block rather than kilograms per session.

**What the evidence actually shows.**

*Two trials tested exactly this in trained men, and they disagree.*

Enes et al. (2024), 31 resistance-trained males, 12 weeks, lower body twice weekly. One group
held its set count; two others added **4 or 6 sets per week every two weeks**. Result: a clean
dose-response **for strength** (6 sets > 4 sets > constant on squat 1RM), and **no between-group
difference for cross-sectional area** — the authors report only a "possible small benefit" for
higher volume, with limited certainty.

Barsuhn/Enes et al. (2024), 55 trained men randomised and 29 completing, 8 weeks, lower body
twice weekly, groups increasing their **own previous** weekly volume by 30% or 60% or maintaining
it. Muscle thickness gains ran **CON 1.07 cm, G30 0.76 cm, G60 0.70 cm** — numerically favouring
maintenance, with the higher-volume arms doing worst.

Neither is decisive: both are small, both lower-body only, both with attrition, and one runs
eight weeks against the other's twelve. But their combined message is not "add sets and grow
more". It is that **at the volumes trained lifters already run, adding sets bought strength and
not size.**

*Both trials started high, and we do not.* Their baselines sit in the 20-plus weekly set region.
`TD-014` prescribes **6.0 fractional sets**. On Pelland's square-root fit that is a different
part of the curve entirely: the marginal set is worth more at 6 than at 22, by the model's own
construction. **The null these trials produce is a null in a region we never enter** — which
makes them a poor argument against volume progression here, and a very good argument against
extending it far.

*Nothing directly tests volume progression from a low baseline.* The dose-response literature is
cross-sectional between arms — group A did 5 sets for eight weeks, group B did 10 — never "group
A walked from 5 to 10 while group B stayed at 5". The inference that walking up the curve
produces the gains the curve predicts is arithmetic, not measurement.

*And the strongest evidence that progressing anything matters is about the other variable.*
Kassiano 2026's progressive arm progressed **load within a rep range at a fixed set count** and
roughly doubled growth. So the best-controlled trial supporting progression supports progressing
the thing this note proposes to replace.

**What it does not settle.**

- **Whether adding sets from a low baseline works.** No trial. The two that exist start high and
  find nothing for hypertrophy.
- **A rate.** "+1 set per muscle per week", "+2 every two weeks" — none compared against another.
  Enes tested +4 and +6 per week, far coarser than anything shippable.
- **A ceiling, and whether it should be personal.** `TD-014` picks 6.0 and
  `references/weekly-set-volume-for-hypertrophy.md` is explicit that no evidence-derived optimum
  exists. A ramp makes the ceiling load-bearing in a way a fixed target does not.
- **What happens at the top of the ramp** — hold, reset, or stop. Nothing says.
- **Whether volume and load progression interact.** Every trial manipulates one and freezes the
  other; doing both at once has never been tested against either.
- **Session time.** These trials supervised whatever the protocol demanded.
  `references/session-time-cost-of-a-set.md` and `TD-012` are the binding constraint here, and no
  trial contains it.

**Where it touches the product.**

- **It makes volume progression defensible for us specifically, on a boundary argument** — 6.0
  sets is below every baseline where adding sets failed, on the steepest part of a curve with no
  plateau. That argument evaporates as a ramp approaches the low twenties.
- **It would make `TD-014`'s number a starting point rather than a target**, which changes what
  that record means and needs a new record rather than a reinterpretation.
- **It collides with `TD-012` and `TD-013`.** Every added set costs about 7.5 minutes against a
  fixed session budget, so a volume ramp and the cut ladder pull in opposite directions on the
  same slots. The ramp has to be subordinate to the budget, or the cut silently undoes it.
- **It is the only progression variable `M1` and `M2` can express**, since neither prescribes load
  at all. If a scheme has to ship before loads do, sets are what there is to progress.
