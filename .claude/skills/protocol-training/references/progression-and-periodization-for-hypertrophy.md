bearing: Periodization beyond "progress week to week" is a null for hypertrophy across four reviews and two meta-analyses — do not build it. Progress; do not periodize.

**What is claimed.** That a programme must progress or growth stops; that progression must
eventually be organised into a periodized structure because linear progression inevitably
stalls; and that when it stalls the programme needs an intervention, usually a deload.

**What the evidence actually shows.**

*Periodization is a null for hypertrophy, and this is the most solid finding in the note.*
ACSM's 2026 overview scores periodization as **does not improve hypertrophy — 4 reviews,
n=1,414, quality of evidence 63%**, and says outright that periodization is "less important than
previously hypothesized". Moesgaard et al. (2022), restricted to **volume-equated** programmes,
agrees: periodization improved 1RM (ES ~0.43) and did not affect hypertrophy. Grgic et al.
(2017) compared the two named models head to head — 13 studies, 303 participants, mean 12 weeks
— and found **SMD -0.02 [-0.25, 0.21]** for muscle growth between linear and daily undulating
periodization.

**Every positive periodization finding in this literature is a strength finding.** Moesgaard's
0.43 is 1RM. Its undulating-beats-linear result held in trained but not untrained subjects, and
again on 1RM. ACSM could not determine the direction for strength at all. Importing any of it
into a hypertrophy product is the exact error this corpus has already caught in exercise order,
variant choice and repetition range.

*Progression itself is a different question, and the corpus's current answer is now contested.*
`references/load-increment-granularity-and-progression.md` records ACSM 2026's line that
progression "is not necessary to achieve beneficial outcomes". That line survives, but it is
bounded twice: ACSM's own periodization sentence begins "with appropriate progressive overload",
so progression is assumed *inside* the comparator, and the qualifier is that overload is "likely
a requirement only for those seeking continued longer term progress".

Against it: Kassiano et al. (2026), 55 untrained young women, **within-subject** (arms randomised
across conditions), 8 weeks of unilateral elbow extension, 3 sets of 8-12, three days a week. The
progressive arm added load on reaching the top of the range; the non-progressive arm held load
and reps fixed for the whole eight weeks. Both grew more than an untrained control arm — **but
the progressive condition produced roughly double the triceps thickness gain at every measured
site.** A within-subject design removes the between-person variance that makes most of this
literature noisy, which makes it stronger evidence than its size suggests.

The honest reconciliation: **progression is not required for *some* growth, and does appear to
change *how much*.** ACSM's statement is about sufficiency; Kassiano's is about magnitude. They
are not in contradiction — but the corpus's current phrasing leans harder on ACSM than the
evidence now supports.

*How progression is expressed matters less than that it happens.* Plotkin 2022 found load and
repetition progression interchangeable for hypertrophy in trained lifters. Scarpelli et al.
(2023), 24 untrained men over 24 sessions, compared a fixed-%1RM progression against an
**RM-zone (double progression)** model: the RM-zone group accumulated volume load at 2.3% per
session against 1.0% and gained more cross-sectional area, with no 1RM difference. It is the only
trial found that compares progression *rules* rather than progression *variables*, it is
untrained, and it favours the rule this product would naturally implement.

*Where progression stops — nobody has measured it.* No trial found tracks time-to-stall. The
closest thing to data is Latella et al. (2024), longitudinal growth modelling of competitive
powerlifters: roughly **7.5-12.5% strength gain in the first year decaying to about 20%
cumulative at ten years**, a smooth decelerating curve with **no inflection points** separating
novice from intermediate from advanced. Practitioner sources put novice linear progression at
"16 weeks to 6 months"; that number has no trial behind it. So the *rate* of progression decays
continuously and is well characterised, while the *event* of a stall is not a documented
phenomenon at all — it is an artefact of demanding a fixed increment from a decaying curve.

And it is a **strength** curve. Nothing establishes that a lift ceasing to add kilograms
coincides with the muscle ceasing to grow; the corpus already holds that load is not the
hypertrophy variable.

*Deload: one trial, and it did not help.* Coleman et al. (2024), 39 resistance-trained young men
and women, nine-week programme, randomised to a **complete one-week cessation at week five** or
to train through. Lower-body thickness increases were equivalent with credible intervals over
zero; the continuous group was **better** on isometric knee extension and squat 1RM. A second
trial in untrained men found no clear benefit either. The planned deload is practitioner
consensus with one direct test against it — and that test used the harsher form, full cessation
rather than reduced volume.

*Autoregulation is real, and a strength effect again.* Hickmott et al. (2022), 15 studies: load
autoregulation against standardised prescription gave **MD 2.07 kg [-0.32, 4.46], p = 0.09 — a
null** — and reported no hypertrophy advantage. The 2025 network meta-analysis ranking
RPE/velocity approaches above percentage-based training ranks them on **1RM only**. The one
Hickmott result touching hypertrophy is volume autoregulation: velocity loss above 25% favoured
CSA (MD 0.61 cm2 [0.05, 1.16]) while 25% or less favoured strength — which is a
proximity-to-failure finding restated, and
`references/proximity-to-failure-and-hypertrophy.md` already owns it.

**What it does not settle.**

- **Whether progression matters in trained lifters over a long horizon.** Kassiano is untrained
  women, eight weeks, one small muscle, one exercise. Its authors call the long-term
  effectiveness of fixed-load training an open question, and nobody has run the non-progressive
  arm past three months.
- **Whether a stall is a signal.** No trial defines one, detects one, or intervenes on one. Every
  stall rule in circulation — three failed sessions, a 10% reset, an unload week — is convention.
- **Whether a *reduced-volume* deload differs from cessation.** Coleman tested cessation; the
  version practitioners actually recommend has never been tested against training through.
- **Whether periodization pays off past 12 weeks.** Pooled durations are 6-12 weeks, and
  periodization is argued for on multi-month horizons the trials do not reach. The null is a null
  *within a block*.
- **Whether autoregulation helps a self-coached lifter.** Every trial is supervised, with a coach
  verifying the RPE. See `references/inferring-proximity-to-failure-from-logged-sets.md`.
- **Nothing here is about deloading for adherence or joint health**, only for adaptation.

**Provenance caveat.** Moesgaard's 0.43 and Kassiano's "roughly double" come from abstracts and
secondary summaries; neither results section was retrieved. **Quote the direction, never the
magnitude**, until the papers are in hand.

**Where it touches the product.**

- **It closes "should we build periodization".** Not for hypertrophy, at our scale. Linear,
  undulating and block models are indistinguishable for growth, and the entire positive
  literature is on a goal this product does not have. A block is a scheduling container, not a
  training claim.
- **It licenses week-to-week progression as a scheme**, and Kassiano is why it is worth having at
  all rather than prescribing a fixed programme.
- **It does not license a stall detector, a deload trigger, or an unload week.** Each would be a
  bet on convention, and `TD-013`'s cut ladder is the wrong template to copy here.
- **`TD-010`'s RIR prescription is not autoregulation**, and nothing here argues for making it so:
  the autoregulation evidence is strength evidence, and the load-autoregulation null is the
  relevant result.
- **It weakens, without overturning, the "progression is not necessary" line** in
  `references/load-increment-granularity-and-progression.md`, which should be read alongside this
  note rather than alone.
