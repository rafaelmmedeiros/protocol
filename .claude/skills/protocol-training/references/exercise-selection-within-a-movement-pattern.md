**What is claimed.** That which exercises fill a slot matters — that a programme needs both
compound and isolation work, that some exercises are better for a muscle than others, and that
rotating exercises produces better results than repeating them.

**What the evidence actually shows.** Almost every version of this question that has been tested
returns a null at the whole-muscle level, and the nulls come from independent groups testing
different variables.

*Compound versus isolation, as whole-muscle growth.* The 2023 meta-analysis of single- versus
multi-joint exercise for limb muscles pooled 7 studies and 10 nested comparisons and found a
trivial standardised point estimate, with moderate precision. Subgroup analysis found no effect
of stratifying by combined SJ+MJ versus MJ alone, and none of volume-equated versus not. Gentil
et al. (2013) is the cleanest primary version: 29 untrained men, 10 weeks, lat pulldown and
bench press alone versus the same plus elbow flexion and extension — elbow flexor thickness rose
6.5% versus 7.04%, peak torque 10.40% versus 12.85%, no between-group difference. **Adding
isolation to a programme that already trains the muscle indirectly has not been shown to add
whole-muscle size.**

*But the muscle has to actually be loaded, and the compound may under-load it.* Mannarino et al.
(2021) is the direct comparison Gentil's design cannot make: unilateral dumbbell row versus
unilateral biceps curl, matched sets to failure, 8 weeks. Elbow flexor thickness rose 11.06%
with the curl and 5.16% with the row. The row grew the biceps, at roughly half the rate. **That
ratio — 0.47 — lands almost exactly on the 0.5 weight the volume literature already assigns an
indirect set** (Baz-Valle 2022, cited in `references/per-muscle-training-frequency.md`). One
trial is not a validation of a convention, but it is the first thing found that gives the
convention an empirical shape rather than an arbitrary one, and it reframes the
compound-versus-isolation question correctly: not *does isolation add something*, but *is the
muscle's fractional volume adequate once the indirect sets are discounted*. Under that framing
Gentil and Mannarino do not conflict — Gentil's MJ-only group got enough biceps volume from
pulldowns and presses to saturate an untrained response; Mannarino's did not, per set.

*Equipment modality is not a growth variable.* Haugen et al. (2023), 13 studies and 1,016
participants, found no hypertrophy difference between free weights and machines (SMD -0.055,
95% CI -0.397 to 0.287, p=0.751, from 5 studies). Strength gains were task-specific in both
directions — free-weight training won free-weight tests (SMD -0.210, p=0.023), machine training
tended to win machine tests (SMD 0.291, p=0.064) — which is a specificity finding, not a
superiority one. Lopes et al. (2019) found elastic resistance produced strength gains comparable
to conventional resistance for upper and lower limb.

*Laterality is not a growth variable.* The 2025 Sports Medicine meta-analysis (9 studies from
703 screened) found no significant hypertrophy difference between unilateral and bilateral
training, and comparable dynamic strength.

*Variation is not a growth variable either, in either direction.* Kassiano et al. (2022)
systematically reviewed 8 studies, N=241, all young men, and concluded that systematic variation
may aid regional adaptations while excessive random rotation may hinder — a conclusion the
review itself frames as inference, not a pooled result. The two controlled trials since point at
a null: Baz-Valle et al. (2019), 19 trained men, 8 weeks, six fixed exercises versus exercises
drawn at random from an 80-exercise database each session — no between-group difference in
vastus lateralis or rectus femoris thickness, no difference in bench or squat 1RM, and the only
site favouring either group favoured the *fixed* group (vastus intermedius). Kassiano et al.
(2025), 70 young women, constant versus varied lower-body exercises across the week — thickness
+7.8-17.7% versus +7.5-19.3%, 1RM +24.4-32.1% versus +29.0-30.1%, "virtually similar" on both.
The one non-null in Baz-Valle 2019 is not hypertrophy at all: the varied group showed a
significant moderate improvement in intrinsic motivation while the fixed group declined
non-significantly. **The strongest case for variety in this literature is adherence, not
growth** — which places it in the same category as `references/cold-start-first-block.md`, not
in the same category as volume.

*Where selection does appear to matter is regional, not whole-muscle.* Fonseca et al. (2014),
49 subjects, 12 weeks, reported that varied lower-body exercise produced more uniform quadriceps
development than back squat alone, with the squat-only condition showing little to no rectus
femoris and vastus intermedius growth despite no difference in total quadriceps CSA. Krause Neto
et al. (2025), 12 studies and 318 participants, found a moderate overall effect of resistance
training on gluteus maximus (SMD 0.71, 95% CI 0.50-0.91) with single-joint (SMD 0.74) and
combined (SMD 0.68) protocols essentially equivalent — again a null between selection
strategies, with the exercise-specific detail sitting under a null at the muscle level.

*The position stand agrees, unusually explicitly.* ACSM's 2026 overview of reviews found that
exercise complexity did not consistently affect training outcomes.

**What it does not settle.**

- **Every pool here is small.** 7 studies, 5 studies, 9 studies, 8 studies. These are convergent
  nulls, not a demonstrated zero, and none has the weight of the 67-study volume
  meta-regression. A real effect of a quarter of a standard deviation would be invisible in most
  of them. This is why the note is `contested` and not `settled`: the direction is consistent,
  the precision is not, and the dissent below is competent.
- **The dissent is about the outcome, not the data.** The regional-hypertrophy camp's position
  is that whole-muscle thickness at one site is the wrong dependent variable, and that Fonseca,
  Mannarino and the muscle-length literature show selection mattering wherever anyone measured
  at a resolution capable of detecting it. That is a live and unresolved methodological argument,
  not a fringe one.
- **Untrained subjects dominate, over 8-12 weeks.** Gentil's is untrained; Kassiano 2025 is
  untrained women; Baz-Valle's trained men is the exception at n=19. Nothing here speaks to
  someone two years in, where the practitioner claim that compounds alone stop being sufficient
  is at its most plausible and least tested.
- **Muscles other than quads, elbow flexors and glutes are barely measured.** The claim that
  presses under-stimulate the lateral deltoid, or that rows under-stimulate the rear deltoid, is
  supported by EMG and by anatomical reasoning, and no training trial found here tests it. Treat
  it as mechanism — but note that `TD-005` splits the deltoid in the schema on exactly this
  reasoning, and therefore rests on mechanism at that point.
- **"Excessive rotation is harmful" has no trial behind it.** Baz-Valle 2019 randomised
  exercises *every single session* from 80 candidates — the most extreme rotation anyone would
  program — and found no hypertrophy cost. The Kassiano 2022 caution is reasoned, not measured.
- **Nothing tests exercise selection under an equipment constraint**, which is the situation a
  real user is actually in.

**Provenance caveat.** Two readings came through secondary summaries. Mannarino's percentages
(11.06% vs 5.16%) could not be opened at source, and the 0.47 ratio drawn from them is the most
load-bearing number in this note — verify before any decision turns on the ratio itself rather
than on the 0.5 convention it corroborates. The regional-uniformity reading of Fonseca 2014 is
also secondary; the primary abstract foregrounds the strength result. The 2023 single- versus
multi-joint meta-analysis is cited by title and DOI without authors because it could not be
opened; resolve before citing it in a record.

**Where it touches the product.**

- **The generator may fill a slot from a small fixed catalogue and defend it.** No evidence
  supports rotating exercises for growth, so the deterministic generator (`ADR-005`) is not
  paying a training cost for being deterministic. This closes exercise rotation as an `M1`
  question.
- **Fractional counting is the mechanism selection acts through, and it is already required.**
  The product-relevant question is never "does this slot need an isolation exercise" but "does
  every modelled muscle group reach its weekly fractional target" — which means secondary
  musculature must be in the schema, and the answer falls out of arithmetic rather than
  judgement. Recorded as `TD-006`.
- **Equipment substitution is cheap.** Free weights, machines, cables and bands are
  interchangeable for growth. This is the evidential basis for `TD-004`: whatever equipment set
  `M1` assumes, a user whose gym differs loses convenience, not hypertrophy — provided the
  movement pattern can still be covered and load progressed.
- **The UI must not rank exercises.** "Best exercise for X" is not a claim this evidence
  supports at whole-muscle level, and it is the exercise-selection analogue of the claim
  `TD-003` already forbids for splits.
