**What is claimed.** Three separate claims, routinely conflated: (a) sets must approach failure
to grow muscle; (b) reaching momentary failure adds stimulus; (c) how close to failure a set
should be depends on the exercise — further on heavy compounds, closer on isolation work.

**What the evidence actually shows.**

*(a) There is a dose-response, and it points toward failure.* Robinson et al. (2024), the only
continuous analysis of the question, meta-regressed **55 hypertrophy and 67 strength studies**
against estimated RIR. Marginal slopes for RIR on hypertrophy were **negative with confidence
intervals excluding the null** — growth increases as sets terminate nearer failure. Strength
showed a **negligible** relationship, with intervals containing zero. The authors are unusually
explicit about their own limits: RIR was *estimated* from training descriptions rather than
prescribed, model fit was "modest," and they describe the analysis as exploratory and urge
caution.

*(b) Reaching failure adds nothing worth having.* Refalo et al. (2023), 15 studies, found a
**trivial** advantage for set failure over non-failure, **ES 0.19 (95% CI 0.00 to 0.37,
p=0.045)**, with no moderating effect of volume load (p=0.884) or relative load (p=0.525), and
concluded there is no evidence that momentary failure is superior. Grgic et al. (2021), 15 RCTs
(~219 participants for hypertrophy), reached the same conclusion, with a modest failure
advantage confined to a trained subgroup (**ES 0.15**). ACSM 2026's practical summary is "close
to failure but not to failure."

*The best-controlled trial is a within-subject one and it is a null.* Refalo et al. (2024): 18
resistance-trained adults, 8 weeks, **contralateral limbs** randomised to failure versus 2-RIR
(leg press) and 1-RIR (leg extension). Quadriceps thickness increased similarly; an 81%
probability of slightly greater rectus femoris growth with **RIR** (+7.38% vs +5.98%), a 79%
probability of slightly greater vastus lateralis growth with **failure** (+7.95% vs +6.59%).
Failure produced higher perceived fatigue and RPE and no growth advantage.

*Where the dose-response breaks down is at high RIR, and here competent people disagree.*
Robinson's model implies 4-6 RIR should grow less than 0-1 RIR. A 2023 trial (n=19 trained,
5 weeks plus deload, squat/bench/deadlift) comparing **0-1 RIR against 4-6 RIR** found no
significant difference in vastus lateralis CSA and similar 1RM gains. It is small, short and
compound-only, so it does not overturn a meta-regression — but it is a direct test in the exact
region the meta-regression is extrapolating into, and it did not find the predicted gap.

*(c) The per-exercise claim has a mechanistic case and no outcome evidence.* Failure costs more
in fatigue: Refalo's scoping review (2022) documents greater neuromuscular fatigue, muscle damage
and post-set discomfort with failure; the neuromuscular fatigue trial found a decrement still
present at **24 h** after failure versus 3-RIR on bench press, with 48 h sufficient for recovery
regardless. Acute fatigue reviews report greater biomechanical decrement, metabolic response and
RPE with failure. And RIR judgement is not uniform: Halperin (2022, 414 participants) found
accuracy improves as reps-to-failure decreases and is statistically indistinguishable below ~12
reps; Steele (2017, n=141) found less experienced trainees underpredict by 4-5 reps versus 1-2
for experienced ones. **What does not exist is a trial comparing a compound-high-RIR /
isolation-low-RIR programme against a uniform-RIR programme for hypertrophy.** The practitioner
position is inference from fatigue cost and safety, and is graded as such.

**What it does not settle.**

- **The shape of the RIR curve.** Robinson's own caveat is that the exact relationship "remains
  unclear." Whether 0 RIR, 1 RIR and 3 RIR differ meaningfully from each other is not resolved;
  the confident part of the finding is that 8 RIR is worse than 2 RIR, which nobody disputes.
- **Estimated RIR is not prescribed RIR.** Robinson's exposure variable was inferred by the
  reviewers from study descriptions. Measurement error here biases in unknown directions.
- **Whether a prescribed RIR is the RIR performed.** Given Steele's 4-5 rep error in novices, a
  "2 RIR" instruction to an unobserved user may land anywhere from 0 to 6. Nothing in this
  literature tells us what an app-prescribed RIR actually produces in the field, and that is the
  gap most relevant to a product that never watches the set.
- **Whether RIR should differ by exercise, `order_class` or laterality is untested.** Both the
  fatigue argument and the accuracy argument are real; neither has been converted into a
  hypertrophy outcome.
- **Failure and injury.** No source found tests injury rates by proximity to failure. The safety
  argument for backing off on a loaded barbell compound is made without a study.
- **Long-run fatigue accumulation is unmodelled.** Every trial is 5-12 weeks with supervision.
  What repeated failure work does across a year of self-directed training is not covered.

**Provenance caveat.** The *direction* of Robinson's slopes and the interval-excludes-null claim
come from the abstract, corroborated across two independent copies. The **numeric slope per RIR
was not obtained.** A decision needing "how much growth does 3 RIR give up versus 1 RIR" cannot
get that number from this note.

**Where it touches the product.**

- **Supports prescribing a moderate, explicit RIR rather than "to failure"** (`TD-010`). The cost
  of 1-3 RIR versus failure is at most trivial (ES 0.15-0.19) and the fatigue saving is
  measurable.
- **Corroborates `references/cold-start-first-block.md` and `TD-001`.** Both the adherence
  argument and the RIR-inaccuracy argument point the same way for a first block, and this note
  adds that the hypertrophy price of backing off is small.
- **It does not, on evidence, authorise a per-`order_class` RIR table.** Where one is built, it
  is convention with a fatigue-and-accuracy rationale, and the record must say so.
- **RIR is a prescribed field on a slot** (`TD-005`), so whatever is chosen is visible to the
  user and must be a number they can act on — itself an argument against fine gradations the user
  cannot distinguish.
