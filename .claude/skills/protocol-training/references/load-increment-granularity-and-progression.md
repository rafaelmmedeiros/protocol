**What is claimed.** That how an exercise is loaded constrains how it can be progressed — a
barbell takes 1.25-2.5 kg steps, a dumbbell rack jumps 2-2.5 kg per hand, a selectorised stack
moves in fixed plates — and that a movement with coarse increments should therefore progress by
repetitions rather than load. And, separately, that a load history built on one variant tells you
something about another.

**What the evidence actually shows.**

*There is a sourced increment rule, and it is a percentage.* ACSM's 2009 progression position
stand: when training at a given RM load, apply a **2-10% increase in load once the trainee can
perform the current workload for one to two repetitions over the target**. That is double
progression, stated by a professional body, and it is the only quantitative increment guidance
found. It is a consensus recommendation, not a pooled result — the stand cites no trial that
manipulated increment size, and no such trial was found.

*Turn that percentage into kilograms and the variants separate sharply.* This is arithmetic, not
evidence, but it is arithmetic against a sourced band:

| Prescription | Smallest step | Relative step | Inside ACSM's 2-10%? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barbell squat, 100 kg | +2.5 kg | 2.5% | yes |
| Barbell curl, 30 kg | +2.5 kg | 8.3% | at the ceiling |
| Dumbbell curl, 10 kg/hand | +2 kg/hand | 20% | no, 2x over |
| Dumbbell lateral raise, 6 kg/hand | +2 kg/hand | 33% | no, 3x over |
| Cable pushdown, 20 kg stack | +5 kg | 25% | no, 2.5x over |

**The pattern is not "free weights versus machines." It is absolute load** — coarse increments
bite hardest where the working weight is smallest, which is almost exactly the `isolation` end of
`TD-005`'s `order_class`. A barbell and a dumbbell are equally fine on a heavy compound and
equally unusable on a lateral raise.

*Repetition progression is not inferior, which is what makes the coarse increment survivable.*
Plotkin et al. (2022): 43 resistance-trained participants (38 completed, at least 1 year of
consistent lower-body training), 8 weeks, back squat, leg extension and calf raises, randomised to
increase load within an 8-12 rep range (LOAD) or hold load and increase reps (REPS), both to
momentary concentric failure. Rectus femoris thickness favoured REPS by 2.8 mm [-0.5 to 5.8];
every other site differed by under 1 mm; squat 1RM favoured LOAD by 2.0 kg [-2.4 to 7.8] against
~20 kg average gains in both; endurance identical. Both intervals cross zero. **For hypertrophy
the two progression routes are interchangeable; for tested strength load progression has a small,
imprecise edge.**

*The 2026 position stand goes further.* Its overview of reviews concludes progression itself "is
not necessary to achieve beneficial outcomes" and is "likely a requirement only for those seeking
continued longer term progress"; load showed no consistent hypertrophy effect (quality of evidence
66%); and periodization was not superior to non-periodized programmes for hypertrophy (QoE 63%, 4
reviews). It also states there were **insufficient data to determine the effects of varied
exercise selection** on either strength or hypertrophy.

*Load history does not transfer across variants — strength is specific, size is not.* Three
independent lines converge. Kassiano et al. (2025): each curl group improved most on its own 3RM.
Nunes et al. (2020): the barbell preacher group gained more peak torque at 20 degrees of flexion
(+39% vs +30%, p=0.046), the angle its variant loads hardest, with no thickness difference. Haugen
et al. (2023): free-weight training won free-weight tests (SMD -0.210, p=0.023) and machine
training tended to win machine tests (SMD 0.291, p=0.064). Smoak (2023) trained 30
resistance-trained men on barbell or dumbbell bench for 12 weeks and reports significant main
effects of time for both 1RMs with no group-by-time interaction — which reads as bidirectional
transfer of *gains* while saying nothing about the absolute loads being comparable, and the
absolute loads are not comparable for straightforward mechanical reasons.

**What it does not settle.**

- **No trial manipulates increment size.** The whole increment argument rests on the ACSM
  percentage band plus arithmetic. Microloading and fractional plates appear only in practitioner
  sources. Treat the 20-33% dumbbell figures as a demonstration that the convention is violated,
  not that violating it costs growth — nothing measured that.
- **Plotkin is 8 weeks, lower body, trained but not advanced, and to failure.** `TD-010` keeps RIR
  at 2-3, which is not the condition tested.
- **Whether a "stalled" lift is a real signal at all.** The 2026 stand's finding that progression
  is not necessary for benefit undercuts the premise that a plateau needs intervention within a
  first block. **Read that line alongside
  `references/progression-and-periodization-for-hypertrophy.md`, which narrows it**: ACSM's claim
  is about *sufficiency*, and a within-subject trial since then found progressing roughly doubled
  growth, which is a claim about *magnitude*. The two are compatible; this note on its own leans
  harder on ACSM than the evidence now supports.
- **Nothing establishes a conversion factor between variants.** There is no evidence-based way to
  say "your 30 kg barbell curl means X on the cable." The absence is total, not merely uncertain.
- **The rotation literature does not specifically test variant-swaps.** Baz-Valle 2019 and Kassiano
  2022/2025 rotated *exercises*; a variant-swap is a strictly smaller perturbation, so the null
  covers it a fortiori. That is reasoning, not measurement.

**Provenance caveat.** Smoak 2023 could not be opened; only the main effect of time and the absent
interaction are reported here, deliberately weakly. Kassiano 2025's magnitudes are secondary-
sourced (see `references/exercise-variant-and-implementation.md`).

**Where it touches the product.**

- **This is the one attribute genuinely bound to the implementation rather than the movement.**
  Everything else a variant might carry is null or unmeasured; the smallest available load step is
  a hard physical property and it differs by a factor of ten across the catalogue.
- **It licenses double progression as the default scheme when progression lands**, with load
  stepping only when the top of the rep range is cleared — ACSM's rule, and Plotkin's
  non-inferiority is what makes the rep-only interval acceptable rather than a compromise.
- **A progression engine must not carry load across variants.** A switch from barbell to cable curl
  resets the load estimate; only volume and muscle credit carry over. Silently inheriting a load
  produces an unperformable prescription — the invisible failure `TD-004` reasons from.
- **It supports no periodized variation of implementation.** Rotating variants across a mesocycle
  has no support in either direction, and ACSM 2026 says the data are insufficient.
- **`M1` prescribes no load at all**, so none of this is consumed yet. `TD-015` records why the
  column is not being added now and what would bring it.
